RCC Brand Guide

A brand is words, images, and experiences. As River Christian Church grows, our art, media, and communication must create impressions that draw people in — never a distraction from leading people to a relationship with Jesus Christ. This guide is the single standard for staff, volunteers, and contractors across web, worship center, print, social, and facility.

Format modeled on brand.life.church — a living web page, not a PDF that goes stale in a folder.

01 — Mission & Voice

Win People · Train Believers · Unleash Disciples

Our voice is warm, direct, and invitational — we write like we're talking to one person, not announcing to a crowd.

02 — Logo & Name

Stable and unchanging

The logo only represents RCC well when used consistently and correctly. Three tiers, chosen by how much brand context the audience already has — not by convenience:

TierLockupUse when
1 — Full logoWaves + RCC + RIVER CHRISTIAN CHURCHFirst-touch and external surfaces: invites, mailers, signage, sponsorships, press, letterhead, anything a person might see with zero prior context. The wordmark does the introducing.
2 — Short lockupWaves + RCCContext-established surfaces: internal comms, campus screens and wall art, social posts on our own accounts, merch, website interior. The community knows us as "RCC" — this tier carries most day-to-day volume.
3 — IconWaves onlyFavicons, avatars, watermarks, and campus decoration — only where tier 1 or 2 appears nearby or the platform shows our name (e.g. a social profile).

Color modes — the website is dark-first, deliberately, with no light-mode toggle. The portal launches dark; revisit only if real Sunday-morning usage complains.

04 — Accent color · DECISION NEEDED

One warm signal color

Teal + dark carries the brand, but every composition needs one signal color for the moment that matters — the featured event, the key stat, the word in the series title. Rules regardless of which we pick: accent is ~10% of a composition maximum, it never replaces teal on primary CTAs, and it always sits on dark or white — never on teal.

On white (print, light sections): full-strength accent is for fills, shapes, and rules only — with navy or white text on top. Accent-colored text on white must use the darker ink variant (AA ≥ 4.5:1): Ember ink #a34c15 (5.8:1) · Gold ink #7a6410 (5.8:1 — note it reads olive, gold's print weakness) · Coral ink #ab2f24 (6.6:1) · Lime ink #55700f (5.7:1). Body text never takes accent — navy always.

CandidateThe case forThe case against
EmberTeal + orange is the classic complementary pair — cinematic on dark, high energy, ages well. "Orange on black" done in a way that still feels like RCC because teal stays dominant.New color — worship graphics and print would adopt it over a season.
River GoldZero transition — it's already the worship-center accent. Warm, hopeful.Lower contrast punch; yellow text on dark needs care at small sizes; reads "softer" than the dark-cinematic direction.
CoralWarm and people-centered; strong on dark.Sits close to red — competes with alert/error meaning in the portal UI.

Recommendation: Ember. It gives the "orange on black" energy inside the teal system, leaves red clean for portal status colors, and — decisive for print — darkens gracefully on white (its ink variant is still recognizably orange, where gold's ink turns olive). River Gold stays available in print during transition. Decide with Heather + Ty at the graphics vision-cast, then it gets locked into the tokens and tagged.

05 — Typography · DECISION NEEDED

Montserrat meets Inter

The 2020 guide names Montserrat as the primary typeface, and it's in every print piece and slide since. The web shell currently runs Inter everywhere. Both are free Google Fonts. Proposed unification: Montserrat for display & headlines (brand continuity across print, slides, and web), Inter for body and UI text (better small-size screen readability, tabular numerals for the portal). Alternative: Montserrat-only, accepting slightly weaker body/UI legibility.

06 — Graphics system

Templates, not one-offs

The full system lives in the Graphics Template System plan; the brand rules it enforces:

RuleStandard
Two tiersNamed annual events (Uncommon, Refresh, Live Nativity, Worship Night) get custom art. Everything else uses its ministry template. Sermon series is its own custom set.
Text-firstNever bake title/date/location into web or app images — Church Center and the website render real text. Every template exports a composed slide and a text-free background.
Resize-safeCenter-anchored, single column, full-bleed backgrounds, no asymmetric composition — so one design fans out to every ratio.
Character capsEnforced at request intake — most quality failures start as too much text.
Wave motifThe wave is the brand's signature shape: section dividers on the web, accents in print. Use the standard wave path — don't draw new waves.
Refresh cadenceCreative input happens at the quarterly template refresh, not per graphic.

07 — Seasonal layer

Stable core, seasonal skin

Sermon series and annual slide sets get room to breathe — the Elevation model: a stable core identity (logo, teal, Montserrat, voice) with seasonal expressions layered on top that expire when the season ends. A seasonal set may bring its own palette and one display font, but it never changes the logo rules, never drops below contrast standards, and never leaks into evergreen surfaces (ministry templates, website chrome, print identity).

Worked example: the 2025 slide templates

The 2025 slide set (spruce #183420, slate #3d5f6d / #607c87, sage #90a99c / #dae0c9; Thunder display + Montserrat) is a good seasonal layer — muted, editorial, distinct. It is not brand core: the sage/spruce family doesn't intersect the teal ramp, and folding it into the core palette would dissolve the one-teal identity this guide just re-established.
